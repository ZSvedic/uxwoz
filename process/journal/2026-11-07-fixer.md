# 2026-11-07: the fixer and its notes

Build step 4 of [the paper canvas plan](../plans/2026-11-06-paper-canvas.md): every 5th pick, and
on *Visit now*, GPT-6.1 Terra visits a mockup, tidies the boxes and redraws up to 5 screens, and a
fourth box lists each change with an Undo. It works and every Undo works, but on a real paper Terra
repairs screens far more than boxes: 2 of 5 planted mistakes on NiceSnore. App code went from 214.8
to 210.4 KB with the cut, and ends at 219.0 KB.

## What was built

1. **The cut**, on its own commit: `firstCopy` and `cutProse`, the format 2 migration and the
   plain-prompt links. A link is `@id`, `@id: its one line`, `back` or `home`; words with no `@`
   lead nowhere, and a file from before names is moved aside as unreadable.
2. **The visit** (`core/src/fixer.ts`): the paper (entries the user wrote marked *(by the user)*)
   and the screens whose newest version is newer than the last visit, parts numbered. The reply's
   lines are parsed into six forms and applied with refusals, as filing's are.
3. **The chain** (`server.ts`): filings and visits share one promise chain per mockup. A reply is
   dropped when a screen, component or the mockup was deleted, or a newer visit asked for, since it
   began. Redraws then run at once as Edits on the screen's own model (`by: 'fixer'`, no filing);
   Start over stops them.
4. **Notes and Undo**: each visit keeps its changes in words, the fixer's note, the refusals, time
   and cost (redraws included), and per change what Undo needs: the removed entry with its place,
   the added ID, or the redraw's version. The fourth box shows them newest first; a Notes button
   in the toolbar counts unseen visits.

## Decisions

- **`add component` takes a where-line** (`… part N: where it belongs`), as filing's form does.
  It does not touch the screen it came from; the fixer's redraws paste it.
- **A merge removes the newer entry** of the two, whichever way the line names them, by place in
  its box. A merge whose newer entry is the user's is refused.
- **Undo is per change and independent.** Undoing an added component leaves the screens a redraw
  made to paste it empty in its place, as Delete does. An undone ID stays retired.
- **The prompt says the limits Terra cannot see.** Without them it asked for 11 redraws, and
  wrote `add component tab-bar …` with a 130-character line; code refused the component while 4
  redraws pasted it, and those screens lost their tab bar. One sentence fixed both: *Every text
  after a colon is at most 120 characters. A visit redraws at most 5 screens: pick the ones that
  matter most.* I also tried *Tidy the boxes first*; it did not help, and went.
- **More cuts** to stay under 220 KB: the canvas's stop on a growing screen, the substitution logs
  and `servedTier`, the spec fields' drop on load, dead frame lines (`document.title`,
  `window.uxwoz`), one CSS rule for one screen's markup, a helper for the nine POST routes, and
  about 8.5 KB of comments that restated code. A subagent listed the candidates.

## The critic's findings

One critic read the full diff. Its two questions first:

- **One kind of app, one failure?** No code line knows one; the two sentences added to the prompt
  restate limits code enforces. *Left*: `MAX_REDRAWS` stays, now called a cost limit in the spec.
- **Not visible on the canvas?** The line above the boxes said *Filing…* during a visit: *fixed*,
  filing counts filings only. Nothing showed which entries the user wrote, though the notes promise
  to leave them alone: *fixed*, a *yours* mark. A dropped visit vanished silently, and a control
  with no `@` did nothing: *fixed*, see 3 and the hint *This control leads nowhere yet*.

Then, fixed with tests unless said:

1. Merging or removing a component blanked every screen that pasted it: now its HTML stays in place.
2. A failed visit moved the last visit's time, so its screens were never read: the next visit counts
   from the last one that did not fail.
3. Any delete in the mockup, even dropping an A OR B, threw away a paid visit. Now only Start over
   or the mockup's delete does, and lines about a deleted screen or entry are refused. This departs
   from the task's *a delete drops the visit*, on purpose.
4. Redraws could land on a screen the user had just edited, and one screen could be redrawn twice:
   now skipped and refused. *Left*: an Edit made while a redraw streams still races it.
5. Undo: a component put back went to the end of the list (a later merge then removed it); an added
   rule the user rewrote was deleted; a version the user already undid made Undo fail forever.
6. A redraw read *redrew* before it ran: it reads *redrawing* until its version comes.
7. A format 3 or 4 screen with no name (an old open A OR B) collided with `home`: it goes at load.
8. *Left*: a 5th pick while a visit runs brings no visit (the next visit reads its screen); Visit now
   costs a call even when nothing changed; the hint says *every 5 picks* apart from `VISIT_EVERY`.

## For step 5, the remakes

- **Count plants on a clean paper and on a real one.** On the paper test's clean paper Terra
  repaired 9 or 10 of 10; on a real NiceSnore it repairs screens first and the boxes seldom. The
  plan's *8 of 10* will not hold on remakes unless the manual makes box tidying as plain as redraws.
  Try that in the manual first, on the held-out dashboard as well.
- **A visit every 5 picks reads 5 screens**; the first visit of an old mockup reads all of them and
  hits the 5-redraw cap at once. Expect junk rules from facts on one screen (*Last night's score is
  34*), and Terra using an unused table instead of removing it.
- **A deleted component's ID never comes back**, so screens that still paste it stay empty until a
  redraw; the fixer tried `add component tab-bar` on every visit and was refused.
- **Room**: 1 KB under 220. The next cuts are listed in the subagent's report (the 25 unused
  exports, the old-device code, about 5 KB more of comments).
- `scripts/fixer-reply.ts <cache> [times] [app]` prints Terra's raw reply to a first visit of a
  cache copy, a cent a run: the quickest way to try a manual change.
