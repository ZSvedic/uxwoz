# 2026-11-02: stacks instead of side-by-side variants

Feedback round after a day off: items 1-5 and 7-9 of the test steps were fine; 6 was a bug, 10 a
new idea for the canvas.

## What changed

- **Info panel (6).** A long prompt in Info made the card wider than its screen, so the bar's tools
  sat outside it. The bar and panels now take the screen's width and never set it; text wraps.
- **Stacks (10).** A row is a stack of its screen's variants, like Time Machine's windows but
  stacked to the right: the pick in front and live, each other variant behind it, 112 px further
  right, shorter and darker, showing only its edge (*Variant B*, its name, *leads to 2 screens*).
  Clicking an edge brings it forward and picks it; the rows below follow, as before.
- **Compare.** A row's **Compare** button fans its stack out side by side in letter order, the pick
  outlined; holding Alt/Option does it for every row (screens forward the key). Letter order, not
  pick-first, so nothing moves under the pointer while picking.
- **First to show goes in front.** Of a new screen's two variants, the first to show text streams
  in front and becomes the pick. No bias to A or B, and the first screen appears sooner. Clicking
  the other's edge mid-stream brings it forward instead.
- **Change** streams its new variant in front and picks it, so the rows below follow it. Refresh
  stays in place; Delete brings the next variant forward.
- The front variant is always the pick, so the old "front" (the pick as it was when the row was
  left) is gone, with the stepped lines it needed: the flow line is straight.

## Decisions

- Stack over side by side: one column, one live screen per step, the pick obvious, the other
  designs one click away. Side-by-side stays one button (or key) away for real comparisons.
- First-to-show over random: as unbiased in practice, and faster.
- Back variants keep their frames loaded, only hidden, so bringing one forward is instant.
